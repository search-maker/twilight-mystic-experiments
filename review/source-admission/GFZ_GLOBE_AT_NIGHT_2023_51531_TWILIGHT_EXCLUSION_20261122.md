## Exact published selection semantics (paper Methods; not inferences)

- The authors started with **83,989 cloud-free reports** and retained **51,351 filtered reports**, for observations January 2011–August 2022.
- They explicitly rejected observations having Sun elevation **> -18 deg** (twilight present). Thus **all 51,351 selected records necessarily have sun depression >=18 deg**, not in the intended first-sighting support window **2–16 deg**. Published selection is deterministic: 0/51,351 eligible for 2–16 *by the authors' filter*. This is an admission exclusion, not a failure rate of observers, and not a result on the unfiltered 83,989 source reports.
- They also rejected Moon above the horizon and any report with an SQM observation. The paper explicitly states that the public database may store an **automatically generated nominal NELM derived from radiometer brightness even with no visual observation**; the public field does not distinguish that from a human estimate. A public NELM row with SQM is therefore **not authenticated human seeing**.
- Globe at Night's participant procedure is **choose the matching sky/constellation chart** and report a naked-eye limiting magnitude (NELM), not timestamped successive NOT_SEEN then DIRECT_SEEN looks of the same catalogued star. The authors subsequently binned the 51,351 into 29,316 monthly geographic cells; those are aggregates, not independent first-star transitions.
