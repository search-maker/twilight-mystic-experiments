## Site, atmosphere and protocol disclosed in the original
- Published observation date: **1990-02-21**; training vessel **Seiun Maru** compass deck, eye height **16 m**; position **36°16.2′ S, 178°09.2′ E** (approx. -36.270000, +178.153333); printed local sunset **18:56**, sunset azimuth **257°**, twilight duration **90 min** (paper p.66, Table 1). Longitude is 178° in the original photographed table, notwithstanding imperfect extracted OCR of that scanned page.
- Clear-sky observation selected; wind SW force 2, air **21°C**, sea **22°C**, smooth sea, lunar age **25.3 days** (p.66, Table 1). **AOD550, aerosol vertical extinction, cloud fraction/opacity measured alongside the star, local artificial-light measurement, observer dark-adaptation time, exact UTC/timezone, stopwatch error and raw prior NOT-seen timestamps: NOT SUPPLIED.** A label of clear weather does not substitute for measured cloud/AOD.
- TOPCON **BM-8** luminance meter declared range **0.01 to 12,000,000 cd m^-2**, plus MINOLTA **T-1M** all-sky illuminance meter declared **0.01–300,000 lx** (p.66). Background sky was measured on a finite **directional grid**, not zenith-only: relative to sunset azimuth 257°, azimuth sectors **0,45,90,135,180°**, sky altitudes **10,30,50,70,90°**; horizon +/-2° luminance separately measured. Full sequence took ~**100 seconds**, which authors estimate displaced solar-center altitude by ~**0.3°** across a sequence. Thus a single grid cannot be assigned to a single exact observation second without additional timestamps.
- Table 5 explicitly labels **星の初認と背景輝度** (first notice/recognition of celestial objects and background luminance); Fig.5 shows Jupiter at Sun 0°, Fig.6 Sirius at -2°, Fig.7 Canopus at -4°, and Fig.8 Achernar/Aldebaran at -6°. This is **visual observer detection**, not only camera/whole-sky instrument, but not a synchronized repeated NO→first YES onset bracket.

**Two necessary reconciliation limits.** (a) The paper directly sampled sky-relative azimuths **0–180°** but gives **three of five object positions beyond 180°** (Jupiter 240.8°, Sirius 202.6°, Aldebaran 271.7°). A reflection/symmetry or contour interpolation may have been used; no authenticated exact ray-to-sensor timestamp/pointing receipt is provided. **Do not treat each star's Table-5 luminance as a directly pointed, simultaneous calibrated ray measurement.** (b) Apparent angular units 28°50.7′ etc. are **degrees plus arcminutes**, not altitude ranges. Solar altitudes are figure stages, not measured onset UTC times; the sky-grid scan's ~0.3° geometric smear must not be silently dropped.
