## Human-star data vs photographic sky (verified in printed page images)

- Printed p.108: the **66 photographic sky pointings**, 1993-05-07 at Sternberg, approximately `V`-band, solar depression `1°–7°`, are **relative-camera background data**, not visual first-star records.
- Printed p.109: the **separately dated human evening star appearances**, August 1992 and February–March 1993, southeast at stellar altitudes **50°–80°**, are reduced to one fit: `m_lim = -1.40(±0.05) + [0.719(±0.007)] * D`. The paper provides **no individual star ID, preceding NOT_SEEN, detection/confirmation UTC, matched AOD/cloud/skyglow or observer adaptation**. The human appearances do **not** share the May 1993 photographic observation date; avoid falsely treating their sky readings as contemporaneous.
- Printed p.110: the author explicitly says `alpha=b/K` **cannot be determined exactly**, partly because star-field brightness distribution delays the observer in finding sparse bright stars. This is a direct original-source warning against interpreting the fit as an uncontaminated detection threshold or minute-accurate first-sighting time.
