## What is newly verified (beyond the prior Byrd 1896 audit)
The existing [first Byrd audit](https://github.com/search-maker/twilight-mystic-experiments/blob/fd93b156c48951a6e4e25507d98d646bfc4312b7/review/source-admission/byrd_1896_ursa_minor_sixth_magnitude_original_source_audit_20261120.md) treated **EST on 1896-10-09 as an uncorroborated assumption**. Going beyond the original single excerpt, the actual publisher-era text says **at the beginning of Chapter VI, printed p.137**, that time in the Moon chapter is "not so uniformly eastern standard time as in the preceding chapters", and flags exceptional astronomical/local times. Chapter V, containing the Oct 9 observation, is one such *preceding chapter*. This is positive author-level evidence for a general **EST convention**; it is **NOT** explicit verification of the October 9 observer's watch setting, watch error, synchronization, minute rounding or exact event-specific UTC. Retain watch-basis residual uncertainty. Original scanned book: https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/0/06/A_laboratory_manual_in_astronomy_%28IA_laboratorymanual00byrdrich%29.pdf ; printed p.137 opening Chapter VI (source-searchable text lines 3614-3622).

**Observer identity:** printed preface p.v explicitly identifies *Miss Abby E. Tucker* as a graduate student making original observations, while printed p.135 signs Oct 9 "A. E. T.". Together these support interpreting those initials as Abby E. Tucker, unlike prior audit initials-only. This is a contextual attribution, not a handwritten 1896 original log or independent consent.

**One additional original published human star appearance report:** printed p.135 §120 describes **July 1, 1896**, sixth-magnitude stars in **Ursa Minor** used by "another observer" to define end of twilight; **1h54m** observed duration after sunset versus **2h12m** calculated. The passage is signed A.E.T., but the *actual star observer* referred to as "another observer" is not identified unambiguously. There is no separately stated watch reading for the first individual star, no negative-to-positive sequence, exact site/direction, measured atmosphere or eye protocol. Do not infer that this is a second eligible event. Printed Table IX on p.132/133 includes July 1 **Back Campus** sunset-duration entry attributed A.E.T. and notes **serious interference by trees**; this is a solar-limb/trees-duration table, not a valid independent timestamp for the star sighting. The July 11 1896 19:13–21:10 example concerns **Milky Way appearance**, not first star.

**October 9 unchanged original report:** Friday 1896-10-09, "Back Campus"; sun upper limb behind hill/trees 17:07; *group* described as sixth-mag Ursa Minor stars became visible 18:32; Milky Way distinct 18:34. No unique catalog star or last-NOT_SEEN check. The book itself cautions that actual sky appearance depends on artificial light, atmosphere and observers' eyes, **without recording those covariates for this evening** (p.134-135).
