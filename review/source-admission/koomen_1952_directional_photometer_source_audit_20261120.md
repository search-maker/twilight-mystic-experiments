## Original field context / physical modalities

- Real 1951 instrumental photometry of twilight **directional sky brightness B**, not human star detection. Sacramento Peak, New Mexico, 2800 m: seven moonless, cloudless evenings in May-June 1951. Rural Maryland, ~30 m, observations January-March 1951. Exact Maryland geographic coordinates and per-table-cell event date/time are absent.
- Nine-stage 1P22 photomultiplier with green filter calibrated to approximate **light-adapted-eye spectral sensitivity**, ~1.5-degree angular field, meridian sweep horizon-to-horizon in 12 seconds. Paper reports site-specific *daytime* vertical sunlight transmission 85%-90% (Sacramento) and 75%-85% (Maryland). These are NOT collocated evening AOD profile measurements.
- Original p.354 explicitly describes >1000 measured values from each station, smoothed versus solar altitude to form Tables I/II. Thus the printed cells are **smoothed published data, not immutable raw observation times**.
- Table coordinates: **H = solar altitude** (negative below horizon); **P = sky pointing altitude**; **Z = relative solar azimuth of sky pointing**. Printed brightness units: candles per square foot. In this small intentionally chosen subset, P = **30 degrees** (not zenith), Z in {0,90,180} degrees, H in {-3,-6,-12,-15} degrees. Both sites. The original table spans +5 to -15 degrees.
- Original authors explicitly warn that **the second half of twilight requires an unperformed spectral correction**, because accurate photopic response was not assured. Late H=-12/-15 numeric cells cannot be promoted into absolutely calibrated late-twilight human visibility proof.
